And just as expected and anticipated, the gaslighting begins.

If you tried to fly anywhere this past weekend, you already know the absolute nightmare that unfolded. On Friday afternoon, the Federal Aviation Administration issued a nationwide ground stop that brought air travel across the entire United States to a dead halt. We’re talking about the longest nationwide grounding since September 11, 2001. This was over four hours of total paralysis that stranded more than 1.5 million travelers, sparked over 10,000 cancellations on Friday alone, and rolled thousands more cancellations right into the weekend (we should watch their stock trades).

For anyone who works with systems, networks, or infrastructure, outages happen. To claim that the systems were incompatible in a production environment is amateur at best. This should have and would have been caught with proper or even basic testing. But what happened next wasn't an honest post-mortem or an acknowledgment of executive responsibility. It was an immediate, shameless scramble to pass the buck.

### The Blame Game in Overdrive

First came the technical scapegoating. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and the FAA pointed fingers at contractor L3Harris, claiming the meltdown started during a telecommunications upgrade at a remote site in Arkansas, where an attempt to switch legacy lines over to fiber overloaded routers with data, knocking out communications across 17 of the nation’s 22 air traffic control centers.

Then came the political deflection.

Over the weekend, Donald Trump finally broke his silence on Truth Social. Did he take ownership of a catastrophic agency collapse happening under his watch? Of course not. He immediately blamed Joe Biden and the Democrats:

> *"The Biden Administration was an FAA disaster. Everything they touched turned bad. Big money spent unwisely, making an old and broken system worse than when they began."*

The official White House accounts joined right in on the theater, putting out statements whining that the Biden administration was allegedly too focused on "inclusive language" to fix air traffic control, claiming Democrats left the FAA’s software in total "disrepair" and failed to modernize it. JFC, seriously?!

And then, right on cue, came the shakedown.

Duffy went on national television to lecture Congress about "years of underinvestment," immediately demanding a $50 billion blank check to overhaul the air traffic control system. Trump echoed the demand minutes later, claiming the system is 50 years old, that they need Congress to cut the check immediately, and that "drips and drabs" won't cut it.

Never mind that Congress had already handed the FAA $12.5 billion for system upgrades in the [Big Terrible Bill](/crises/big-bill), or that Duffy was asking for $30 billion just two months ago in September before suddenly jacking the price tag up by another $20 billion the minute the screens went dark. They can't even give a straight technical explanation for why a single router upgrade cascaded into a nationwide total failure, but they sure know how to ask for 50 billion taxpayer dollars while pointing fingers across the aisle.

### The Hypocrisy is Unreal

Here is the reality that Trump, Duffy, and the rest of this administration are desperately hoping the public forgets:

They have full, unchecked control.

They hold the Oval Office. They hold the House. They hold the Senate.

They have had full control and instead of actual governance, they installed loyalists and yes-men with no experience and shit the bed as usual. Then suck their thumbs and point fingers when their incompetence fails in usual spectacular fashion.

You don't get to run the entire federal government, oversee the single worst domestic aviation failure in twenty-five years, and then cry that the previous guy who hasn't been in office for two years is responsible for your agency crashing into a wall on a Friday afternoon.

This wasn’t a legacy hangover. This was a catastrophic failure under *their* watch and *their* tenure.

They own the government and its agencies; they own the blame.
